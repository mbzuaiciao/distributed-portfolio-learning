# Tutorial 23: Epistemic Exposure Scaling vs Realized Volatility Targeting

In quantitative finance, the prevailing standard for adjusting aggregate portfolio risk is **realized volatility targeting** (or risk budgeting). Before claiming that Bayesian epistemic uncertainty provides useful exposure timing, we must benchmark it against this classical standard.

---

## 1. Classical Realized Volatility Targeting ($M_6$)

Realized volatility targeting scales total portfolio exposure inversely with recent historical volatility:
$$s_{\text{vol}, t} = \min\left(1.0, \, \frac{\sigma_{\text{target}}}{\hat{\sigma}_{\text{hist}, t}}\right)$$

Where:
- $\sigma_{\text{target}}$ is the target annualized volatility (e.g., $15\%$ or $20\%$).
- $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{hist}, t}$ is the rolling sample standard deviation of weekly portfolio returns over a lookback window of $L = 12$ weeks, annualized by $\sqrt{52}$:
  $$\hat{\sigma}_{\text{hist}, t} = \sqrt{52} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{L-1} \sum_{k=1}^L \left(r_{\text{base}, t-k} - \bar{r}\right)^2}$$
- **Zero Lookahead Constraint**: $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{hist}, t}$ is computed strictly using returns up to week $t-1$. At week $t$, $r_{\text{base}, t}$ is unknown.
- **Unleveraged Bound**: Maximum leverage is capped at $1.0$ ($s_{\text{vol}, t} \le 1.0$), ensuring a pure long-only portfolio with cash.

---

## 2. Epistemic Exposure Scaling ($M_5$)

In contrast, epistemic exposure scaling uses internal model uncertainty rather than backward-looking market returns:
$$s_{\text{epi}, t} = \frac{1}{1 + \lambda_{\text{epi}} u_t}$$

Where $u_t$ is the 75th percentile of asset-level epistemic standard deviations generated by the Bayesian-LoRA Monte Carlo ensemble.

### Fundamental Differences:
| Feature | Volatility Targeting ($M_6$) | Epistemic Scaling ($M_5$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Information Source** | Past realized return variations (market prices) | Latent epistemic disagreement (model weights) |
| **Temporal Nature** | Purely backward-looking (historical sample variance) | Forward-looking model parameter dispersion |
| **Correlation with $u_t$** | Low ($\text{Spearman } \rho \approx 0.12 - 0.27$) | Independent signal source |
| **Sensitivity** | Reactive: spikes after drawdowns have begun | Proactive: responds to novel or unobserved inputs |

---

## 3. The Combined Scaling Rule ($M_7$)

To evaluate whether epistemic uncertainty and realized volatility provide complementary risk-reduction signals, we introduce the combined policy:
$$s_{\text{combined}, t} = \min\left(s_{\text{epi}, t}, \, s_{\text{vol}, t}\right)$$

Under $M_7$, the portfolio de-risks whenever **either**:
1. Recent market volatility is unusually high, **or**
2. The neural network exhibits elevated epistemic uncertainty.

---

## 4. Empirical Correlation & Regimes (Holdout Findings)

Across the 69-week out-of-sample holdout (2024–2025):
1. **Low Cross-Correlation**: The Spearman rank correlation between historical volatility $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{hist}, t}$ and aggregate epistemic uncertainty $u_t$ is moderate:
   $$\rho(u_t, \hat{\sigma}_{\text{hist}, t}) \in [0.124, 0.275]$$
   This proves that epistemic uncertainty is **not merely a smoothed proxy for historical volatility**.
2. **2x2 Macro-Regime Breakdown**:
   When dividing the timeline into median splits of uncertainty and volatility:
   - **Low $U$ / Low Vol**: Highest weekly base return ($+1.33\%$ to $+1.64\%$).
   - **Low $U$ / High Vol**: Flat or negative return ($-0.38\%$ to $+1.44\%$).
   - **High $U$ / Low Vol**: Subdued return ($+0.09\%$ to $+0.75\%$).
   - **High $U$ / High Vol**: Moderate return ($+0.91\%$ to $+1.37\%$).
